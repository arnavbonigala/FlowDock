Learn it once. Everything works this way.

Music, a volume change, a download and a file all move through the same four states, so the next thing FlowDock shows you behaves like the last one.

Idle

Nothing to say, nothing to show.

The surface matches the cutout to the pixel, so an idle Mac looks like a Mac with nothing running. No glyph, no dot, no badge waiting for you to notice it. On a display without a notch, FlowDock hides itself.

Contextual

The value you just changed.

Press the volume key and a pane of glass arrives under the notch, then clears a second and a half later. Brightness, keyboard backlight and the mic read the same way. The notch itself stays put.

Persistent

What's playing, while it plays.

Album art on one side, a live spectrum on the other, and the title scrolling between them while it plays. Downloads and timers take the same shape, and each one leaves when it finishes.

Expanded

Drag a file up. It opens.

Hold a file over the notch and the shelf opens under your cursor. Preview it, compress it, convert it, lift the background out, AirDrop it, then drag it on to wherever it was going. Your file stays where you left it on disk.

Opening it the first time.

FlowDock ships without an Apple Developer ID, so macOS checks with you once. You can read the workflow that built this release and rebuild it yourself from the tag.

  1. Open the DMG and drag FlowDock to Applications. Try to open it, then dismiss the warning. macOS quarantines anything it cannot check with Apple.
  2. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll to Security. A line names FlowDock. Click Open Anyway.
  3. Confirm. Every later launch is normal.
Prefer the terminal? xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/FlowDock.app

FlowDock updates itself after that. It reads a signed feed and installs what you approve, downloads nothing else, asks for no account and sends no analytics.
